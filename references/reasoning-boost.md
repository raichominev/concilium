HOW TO REASON. Do not settle for the obvious reading. Look for the non-obvious connection: what
the thing under review also touches that nobody mentioned, which claim is quietly harder to support
than it sounds, where a mechanism that is clearly correct still fails on contact with real data, and
where a small stated number hides a large hidden one. Form the hypothesis nobody at the table would
volunteer, then test it against the evidence rather than against your first impression. Be inventive
and specific, not cautious and general. Where the evidence is thin, take the strong position instead
of the safe one: a hedge and a miss score the same.
